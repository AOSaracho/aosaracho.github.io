Research

JMP

Job-market paper · with Jiri Havel

Inventories, Trade Credit, and Imported Inputs in Firm Dynamics

We study how firms finance imported-input purchases when supplier distance, inventory needs, and financial constraints shape the choice between bank credit and trade credit. Using confidential Belgian monthly customs transactions linked to firm balance sheets, we find that bank credit and trade credit are substitutes for financially constrained firms.

The evidence shows that importers rely more on bank credit relative to trade credit when suppliers are farther away. We develop a dynamic importer model in which trade credit is an intertemporal financing choice for imported-input purchases.

Preliminary draft available on request · Expected full draft Fall 2026

02

Working paper · with Kazuma Inagaki

Demographic Divergence and the Rise of Exporter Participation

Over the past four decades, U.S. labor-force growth and business formation have fallen, the share of establishments belonging to exporting firms has risen, and U.S. labor-force growth has fallen behind that of its trading partners. We study these facts in a two-country model with heterogeneous firms, sunk export costs, endogenous entry and exit, and international borrowing. In a symmetric world, slower labor-force growth leaves firms’ export and exit decisions unchanged on a balanced growth path and raises participation only by shifting the population of firms from entrants toward incumbents. When labor-force growth differs across countries, relative market size and international borrowing add a second channel that works through the real exchange rate. In an accounting of U.S. data over 1978–2022, labor-force growth accounts for about a third of the movement in exporter participation and about half of the movement in entry, but for little of the movement in exports; trade frictions account for most of the rest. More than half of demography’s effect on participation comes from the demographic gap with trading partners, which a symmetric model cannot capture, and more than half of that works through borrowing.

Presented at ETSG 2025 · Download paper (PDF) ↗

03

Work in progress

Disclosed Supply-Chain Risk, Inventory Adjustment, and the Trade-Credit Channel

Using 136,056 SEC filings from 2018–2024, I construct measures of supply-chain, trade-credit, and working-capital risk. Supply-chain risk language predicts future inventory accumulation, while industry-level trade-credit risk predicts firms’ payables adjustment.
